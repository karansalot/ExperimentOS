# Three-minute interview demo script

Setup before the call: `make api` and `make web` running, browser at `http://localhost:3000`, scope set to **Full dataset**, simulator reset to defaults. Spoken text is in quotes; actions are in brackets. About 430 words at a normal pace.

---

**0:00 – The problem (20 s)** · *[Insights page]*

"ExperimentOS answers a question every growth team has: did the treatment work, how sure are we, and who should get it? It runs on the public Criteo uplift dataset, 14 million users from real advertising holdout tests. Nothing here is mocked: each number is computed by a pipeline and cached with a dataset checksum and a seed. This page is generated by the platform itself: ten findings, each linked to its evidence."

**0:20 – The effect, with uncertainty (35 s)** · *[click the first insight, then the three-estimator chart on Experiment overview]*

"Treatment lifts visit rate by 1.03 percentage points, tight interval. But look at this chart. The raw difference is the top dot. The two below adjust for user features, and they are about 28% lower. Why? Because the two arms aren't perfectly comparable in this file."

**0:55 – Diagnostics (35 s)** · *[Statistical diagnostics]*

"Sample ratio check passes: exactly 85/15. But covariate balance fails a strict test. Every difference is small, yet a classifier predicts assignment with AUC 0.51 instead of 0.50. So I report adjusted estimates alongside the raw one rather than hide it. Below are segment effects with false-discovery control across 45 tests. One segment of this feature has a 3.5-point effect, 2.6 after covariate adjustment; the others are near zero."

**1:30 – Who responds (35 s)** · *[Uplift model lab, then scroll to Explainability]*

"Five targeting strategies, trained on 60%, selected on 20%, reported on a final 20% they never saw. The top 10% of users get a six-point lift versus 0.7 on average. Honest finding: the uplift model beats a plain response model only slightly. This column is a paired bootstrap of the difference, so I can say that with a confidence interval. For conversions, the fancy models are worse. And this scatter shows the model's predicted segment differences sit on the diagonal of what the data shows: correlation 0.99."

**2:05 – The decision (40 s)** · *[Targeting simulator; drag coverage, then change cost to 0.1]*

"Now business assumptions, which are mine, not the data's, and labelled that way. At five cents per user and five per visit, treating everyone loses money; treating the top 14% makes about 25 thousand per million users. That cut-off is chosen on a validation split and measured on a separate test split, and here is the rule in deployable form: predicted uplift of at least 0.8 points. *[change cost]* Double the cost and the recommended coverage shrinks. The heat map shows where the conclusion flips."

**2:45 – Wrap (15 s)** · *[Decision brief, click Download]*

"The brief turns that into a stakeholder read-out with limitations, exportable as Markdown. Behind it: 20 SQL queries in DuckDB, 108 tests that check the statistics against known ground truth, and CI. There is also a planner that sizes the follow-up experiment."

## Likely follow-up questions

- **"Why is the adjusted effect lower?"** The file pools several tests and was sub-sampled non-uniformly for privacy, so features correlate weakly with assignment. Doubly-robust and regression adjustment agree with each other, which is why I trust the direction of the correction. I cannot rule out imbalance on things I can't observe.
- **"How do you know the estimators are right?"** The test suite simulates data with a known effect and confounded assignment: the naive difference is biased, and both adjusted estimators recover the truth. Primitives are checked against statsmodels and SciPy, and confidence-interval coverage is checked by simulation.
- **"How do you prevent leakage?"** Hash-based splits on row position, fit on train only, champion chosen on validation, cross-fitted evaluation scores, and a test that no training row appears in the prediction file.
- **"Why not a head-of-file sample for development?"** The file is ordered in single-arm blocks; the first 5 million rows are all treated. A SQL query in the catalog shows it. The dev sample is a hash sample and is labelled as a sample on every page.
- **"What would you do next?"** Run a prospective test of the targeting policy itself, sized with the planner page, and get real cost and value inputs.
- **"Isn't the recommended coverage overfit?"** It is chosen on the validation split by the risk criterion, and the profit shown comes from the test split, which played no part in choosing it.
